## 1. Schindler (schindler.com)

### A. Brand Identity
- Distinctive identity created through original photography (real installations, real buildings), a clean geometric logo, and a premium but serious color palette (blue + white rather than generic dark). The identity is built primarily through imagery quality and structured typography rather than decorative effects.
- Personality: serious engineering company that also cares about passenger experience. Not playful, not luxury — confident and technical.

### B. Storytelling
- Visitor understands first: "We are a leading elevator company with a comprehensive product portfolio."
- The homepage guides attention through a clear hierarchy: hero statement → product categories → modernization focus → project evidence → digital solutions → company story → conversion.
- No repetitive card patterns. Each product family has its own structured presentation.

### C. Typography
- Large bold headings with tracking-tight. Uppercase eyebrow labels. Clean sans-serif family.
- Technical terms (geared, gearless, MRL, destination dispatch) used directly without simplification.
- Spec information presented in structured lists rather than dense paragraphs.

### D. Layout
- Sections clearly separated. No excessive decorative borders. Content is organized by building application (residential, office, hospital, transport) in addition to product categories.
- Avoids the standard "hero + 3 cards + testimonials" template by using a multi-layer navigation with categorized products, modernized service sections, and real project showcases.

### E. Media
- Real project photography dominates (General Motors Building modernization, stadium installations, high-rise projects). No generic decorative imagery.
- Product images appear to be actual installations rather than stock photos.
- Technical diagrams (ReStore/ReNew/RePlace modernization steps) explain complex services clearly.

### F. Product / Technical Communication
- Product pages explain applications clearly: "low-rise," "mid-rise," "high-rise," with capacity ranges and use cases.
- Technical specifications presented as structured lists with clear headings.
- Modernization services broken into specific solutions (ReStore, ReNew, RePlace) rather than generic "we modernize."

### G. Trust & Evidence
- Manufacturing evidence visible: Clinton (NC) and Hanover (PA) facilities documented with square footage, LEED certification, and quality processes.
- Certifications displayed (ISO mentioned in manufacturing section).
- Project portfolio with named buildings, locations, and dates.
- Team presence through career profiles (Dallas mechanic with 21 years experience).
- News/articles provide evidence of current work (modernization contracts, engineering challenges).

### H. Interaction & Motion
- Navigation is clean and structured. No excessive decorative animation visible from homepage inspection.
- Interactive elements (search, navigation, product selector) serve navigation, not decoration.

### I. Conversion
- Primary conversion: "Contact us" / "Configure your solution" / direct phone number visible in header.
- Calculator/configurator linked (SchindlerPlan.com) for architect/engineer use.
- Service contract selector available.
- The site guides toward contact or specification download rather than pushing a single sales funnel.

### J. Mobile
- Structure preserved on mobile: navigation collapses cleanly, sections stack vertically, technical content remains accessible, conversion paths remain visible.

### Deenar Lesson: What They Did → Why It Works → What Deenar Could Learn
| Observation | Why It Works | Deenar Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Original photography of real installations and facilities | Builds immediate credibility without marketing language | Deenar must replace all Unsplash images with real factory/project/team photos when M2.5 provides them |
| Manufacturing evidence (facility names, sq ft, certifications) | Turns "manufacturer" claim into verifiable fact | Deenar should document factory facility with similar specificity once confirmed |
| Product categories organized by building application (residential, office, hospital) | Makes selection intuitive for non-technical buyers | Deenar's existing 6-family structure should be presented with application context |
| Modernization broken into named solutions (ReStore, ReNew, RePlace) | Complex service made understandable through naming and structure | Deenar's modernization service should use clear, named upgrade categories |
| Technical specs presented as structured lists with headings | Engineers can scan; non-technical buyers can understand categories | Deenar's existing spec data should remain structured — expand with real dimensions |
| Project portfolio with named buildings, dates, locations | Evidence is specific and verifiable rather than aspirational | Deenar needs 8–12 real project entries with similar specificity |
| Engineer/career profiles with real names, years of experience, roles | Humanizes technical company; builds accountability | Deenar needs team bios after M2.5 verifies key people |
| Configurator for architects (SchindlerPlan) | Professional audience gets technical tools; builds credibility | Deenar's calculator serves similar role — should remain prominent |

## 2. Otis (otis.com)

### A. Brand Identity
- Identity created through strong navigation structure, organized categories, and technical resource emphasis rather than decorative design. The blue/red brand colors serve functional purposes (navigation categories, service sections, CTAs). The identity is institutional and authoritative — appropriate for a global infrastructure company.
- Personality: highly professional, technically authoritative, globally present.

### B. Storytelling
- Visitor understands: "We are a global leader with comprehensive products, services, modernization, and digital solutions. We serve architects, developers, owners, and passengers."
- The homepage uses strong category organization (BY PRODUCTS, BY INDUSTRY, SERVICE, TOOLS & RESOURCES) rather than a linear scroll story. This works for audiences who know what they need and want to navigate directly.

### C. Typography
- Clear heading hierarchy with large statements ("We are the global leader in the manufacture..."). Supporting statistics presented prominently ($14.4B, ~2.5M units, 2.5B people moved daily).
- Clean sans-serif family. Technical terms presented directly.

### D. Layout
- Multi-level navigation with categorized dropdowns. The site avoids repetitive card patterns by organizing by both product type and building/industry application.
- Sections clearly separated. No decorative effects overpowering content.

### E. Media
- Real installation photos used (stadium elevators, infrastructure projects, passenger experience photos). Not decorative.
- Product imagery shows actual applications.
- Technical resources include BIM models, CAD drawings, AIA courses — real professional tools.

### F. Product / Technical Communication
- Products organized by rise height (low-rise, mid-rise, high-rise, heavy-duty) — a practical engineering classification.
- Technical resources explicitly categorized for architects/engineers, contractors/developers, building owners/managers, and passengers.
- Modernization services broken into clear upgrade categories.

### G. Trust & Evidence
- Statistics prominently displayed on homepage (global scale numbers).
- Real global projects shown with photos and descriptions (Oriental Pearl Tower, Eiffel Tower, cruise ships, stadiums, infrastructure).
- Manufacturing/service evidence implied through global reach description.
- News section provides current work evidence.
- Certifications and compliance mentioned in footer.

### H. Interaction & Motion
- Navigation is the dominant interactive element. The site relies on structured menus rather than scroll animations.
- Mobile menu collapses cleanly with categories preserved.

### I. Conversion
- Multiple clear CTAs: "CALL US" (prominent number), product finder, service contracts, modernization inquiries, resource downloads.
- The conversion path supports different audience types rather than forcing one funnel.

### J. Mobile
- Category structure preserved. Navigation collapses. Stats and key messages remain visible. Technical resources section remains accessible.

### Deenar Lesson: What They Did → Why It Works → What Deenar Could Learn
| Observation | Why It Works | Deenar Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Category organization by both product AND industry/application | Different audiences find relevant content quickly | Deenar's 6 products + 4 services could be cross-referenced with audience types (residential, commercial, industrial, healthcare) |
| Technical resources categorized by audience (architects, contractors, owners, passengers) | Professional audiences get direct access to relevant tools | Deenar could create a resources/downloads section once technical documents exist |
| Global projects shown with names, photos, locations | Evidence is specific, verifiable, and impressive | Deenar needs a project portfolio structured this way after M2.5 |
| Homepage statistics prominently displayed ($14.4B, 2.5B people daily) | Numbers create immediate credibility | Deenar must never invent such statistics; any numbers shown must be verified first |
| Multiple conversion paths (call, find office, service contract, modernization) | Different buyers have different urgency levels | Deenar's WhatsApp + calculator + survey structure is appropriate; maintain this flexibility |
| News/current work section shows company is active | Evidence of ongoing work builds credibility | Deenar's blog serves similar purpose — expand with verified case studies and real news |

## 3. Arup (arup.com)

### A. Brand Identity
- Identity created through original photography (engineers, buildings, projects, people), structured typography, and clean layout — not through decorative effects. The red logo is minimal and geometric. The identity feels serious, global, and expertise-driven.
- Personality: confident engineering expertise combined with human presence (team profiles, diverse people, real projects).

### B. Storytelling
- Visitor understands: "We shape a better world through engineering. We have 80 years of experience. Our people solve complex problems globally."
- The story progresses through identity statement → project evidence → people/profiles → issues/insights → services → projects → conversion.
- Real people are shown prominently (Paula Acosta, Martin Swaffield, Chandan Joshi, Alex Price, Brittany Moffett) with names, titles, brief quotes.

### C. Typography
- Large, bold headings with clean sans-serif family. Uppercase eyebrow labels. Structured sections with clear headings.
- Technical content (issues, insights) uses structured headings and paragraphs without decorative interruption.
- Profile cards show names prominently with role and brief quote.

### D. Layout
- Project cards use real photos with project names and locations. No decorative card styles overpowering content.
- Sections clearly separated. No repetitive patterns. Each section has a distinct purpose and visual treatment.
- The homepage avoids standard template patterns by combining identity, project showcase, people profiles, and issue-based content in a single flowing layout.

### E. Media
- Real photography dominates: Sydney Opera House, Little Island, Peru reconstruction, Sagrada Familia, staff members, buildings under construction.
- No decorative stock imagery. Every photo serves a purpose: identity, evidence, or people.
- Technical content presented through structured text rather than complex decorative graphics.

### F. Product / Technical Communication
- Services described through market categories (planning, design, engineering) rather than product families.
- Issues section explains complex challenges (high-speed rail, energy grid, urban cooling) with real context.
- Technical credibility established through real projects, people, and structured insights.

### G. Trust & Evidence
- Team profiles with real names, roles, and quotes — the most prominent trust element.
- Project portfolio with named projects, locations, and descriptions.
- Company history mentioned ("For 80 years...").
- Issues/insights demonstrate expertise through thought leadership.
- Diversity shown naturally through profile selection.

### H. Interaction & Motion
- Minimal decorative motion visible. Navigation works cleanly. Focus appears on content and navigation rather than decorative effects.

### I. Conversion
- Clear conversion paths: "See our projects," "Careers," "Contact us," direct navigation to services/markets.
- The site does not feel like a sales funnel — it feels like an expertise organization that invites collaboration.

### J. Mobile
- Content preserves hierarchy. Navigation collapses cleanly. Project cards stack. Profile cards remain readable. The story structure remains intact.

## 5. What Deenar Should Learn (Transferable Principles)

### Strongly applicable (should directly inform M4 design)

1. **Evidence should dominate imagery.** (From Schindler, Arup, Otis) Every major section should prioritize real evidence over decorative imagery. The design architecture must include structured placeholders for project photos, factory images, team profiles, and certification displays.

2. **One strong visual identity carries more weight than many decorative effects.** (From Schindler) The custom HeroLift SVG is Deenar's strongest original asset. The design should build around this and similar original elements rather than distributing decorative gradients across many cards.

3. **Structured technical information creates credibility.** (From Schindler, Otis) Product specs, calculator outputs, service descriptions, and process steps should remain clearly structured — not buried in marketing copy.

4. **Team and project evidence builds trust faster than abstract claims.** (From Arup) Real engineer profiles, completed installations, and named references provide credibility that no marketing statement can replace.

5. **Category organization supports navigation.** (From Otis) Deenar's product and service categories should be clearly organized by both product type and audience/building application.

6. **Conversion paths should serve different urgency levels.** (From Schindler, Otis) WhatsApp (quick enquiry) + calculator (budget planning) + site survey (formal quote) creates a flexible conversion system that serves different buyer stages.

### Potentially applicable (may work depending on M2.5 evidence)

7. **Real manufacturing documentation.** (From Schindler) Once M2.5 confirms factory capability, manufacturing evidence sections (facility photos, fabrication process, quality documentation) should be designed with the same structured seriousness as Schindler's manufacturing pages.

8. **Project portfolio with named buildings.** (From Schindler, Arup) Once M2.5 provides 8–12 real projects, a portfolio section should use the same format: name, location, year, brief description, real photo, link to details.

9. **Technical resources for architects/engineers.** (From Otis) Once technical drawings, datasheets, and component specifications exist, a resources/downloads section should serve professional audiences directly.

10. **Modernization presented as named upgrade paths.** (From Schindler) Deenar's modernization service could use named upgrade categories (similar to ReStore/ReNew/RePlace) once modernization evidence exists.

11. **News/current work section.** (From Schindler) Once Deenar has verified recent work or industry insights, a news/blog section should expand with real case studies and verified project updates rather than generic guides.

### Not applicable (should be avoided for Deenar)

12. **Schindler's luxury showroom aesthetic.** Schindler's premium presentation includes luxury showroom imagery and high-end design language. Deenar's current positioning is engineering/manufacturing credibility — not luxury. The design should not adopt luxury visual language that conflicts with Deenar's confirmed identity.

13. **Otis's massive global statistics.** Otis displays statistics like "2.5 billion people moved daily" and "2.5 million units serviced." Deenar does not currently have verified statistics of this scale. Any statistical display must use only verified, documented figures.

14. **Arup's abstract/conceptual imagery.** Arup uses abstract architectural and conceptual imagery that serves its design consultancy identity. Deenar's identity is manufacturing and engineering — the imagery should remain concrete (real lifts, real factories, real installations) rather than abstract.

15. **Generic AI-pattern structures copied from any reference.** None of the references should be copied as templates. Their value is in transferable principles, not in exact layouts, colors, or component structures.

## 6. What We Should NOT Copy

### From Schindler
- The premium luxury showroom aesthetic (Schindler's high-end design language is appropriate for a global luxury brand, not for Deenar's engineering/manufacturing identity).
- The exact color palette or gradient treatments (Schindler uses blue gradients; Deenar uses cyan on dark — the function is similar but the execution must remain Deenar's).
- The globalization message (Schindler's "150 years" / "global reach" messaging requires verified history and scale; Deenar's claims must remain verified).

### From Otis
- The institutional/corporate visual structure (Otis's site serves a massive global audience with complex navigation; Deenar's audience is more focused — the design should remain focused, not over-structured).
- The statistical claims format (any statistics shown must be verified through M2.5; no invented numbers).
- The multi-level dropdown navigation (Deenar has 6 pages; over-complex navigation would add friction rather than help).

### From Arup
- The abstract/conceptual imagery approach (Arup's design identity relies on abstract architecture and conceptual thinking; Deenar's identity requires concrete manufacturing and installation evidence).
- The extensive profile/card structure (Arup's team profiles work because they have verified team members; Deenar should only show profiles after M2.5 confirms team members).
- The issue-based content framework (Arup's "issues" work because they have verified expertise and research; Deenar should expand blog content only with verified insights, not aspirational issues).

## 7. Preliminary Design Hypotheses for M4.2

These are hypotheses — not final rules — that should guide the future `docs/design.md`.

1. **Distinctive identity requires original evidence, not decorative effects.** The redesign should prioritize real factory/project/team photography over decorative gradients and stock imagery.

2. **One original visual moment carries more weight than many decorative elements.** The HeroLift SVG is Deenar's strongest original asset. The design should feature it prominently rather than distributing small decorative effects across many sections.

3. **Evidence placement should follow the visitor journey.** Manufacturing evidence should appear after product identity is established. Project evidence should appear after pricing/process are explained. Team evidence should appear after services are described.

4. **Technical information requires structured presentation.** The existing spec format should be preserved and expanded with real dimensions, component details, and documentation links when available.

5. **Pricing transparency must be visually reinforced.** The calculator output should clearly show "ESTIMATE" labels, breakdown structure, and disclaimers — not appear as a final quote.

6. **Service coverage must be visually unambiguous.** Any service section must clearly state verified geographic coverage and response commitments — resolving the current contradiction rather than hiding it.

7. **Mobile preservation of story hierarchy is essential.** The calculator, product specs, pricing breakdown, conversion paths, and evidence sections must remain fully understandable on mobile screens.

8. **Industrial identity should feel confident, not cold.** Real team members, practical conversion paths, and clear language should balance the dark industrial palette with human accessibility.

9. **Premium presentation should come from precision, not luxury decoration.** Clean typography, structured data, careful spacing, original illustrations, and real evidence create premium credibility — not abstract luxury effects.

10. **Every visual decision must serve the M3 story.** The design should support SEE (identity), UNDERSTAND (range/process/pricing), TRUST (evidence/services), and ACT (conversion) — not add visual elements that serve no story function.

11. **Placeholder architecture should accommodate missing evidence.** Sections designed for factory photos, project portfolios, team profiles, and certification badges must be built so that real evidence can be inserted without restructuring — maintaining credibility both before and after M2.5.

12. **The redesign should feel like an engineering company that is becoming more transparent, not a marketing company becoming more impressive.** Every design improvement should increase the site's ability to show what Deenar actually does — not just what it claims to be.
